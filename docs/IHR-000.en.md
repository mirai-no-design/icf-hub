## 1. Purpose

IHR (ICF Hub Request for Comments) is a document series that accumulates the specifications, conventions and operating rules of ICF Hub as public documents owned by no one.

IHR follows the conventions of the Internet's RFCs:

- anyone can read them, free of charge
- anyone can comment (as the name says, requesting comments is the whole point)
- the record of discussion is public
- no company or organization owns them
- once published, a document is never silently altered; changes accumulate as new documents or revisions

Open publication itself is a defensive publication (creation of prior art), protecting the specification as a commons from enclosure by third parties.

## 2. Numbering

- Numbers take the form `IHR-NNN` (three digits, zero-padded), issued in ascending order from 000
- Numbers are never reused; withdrawn numbers remain as gaps
- IHR-000 (this document) defines the process itself; IHR-001 onward are substantive specifications
- Numbers are assigned by the Editors when a draft is accepted; numbers cannot be reserved

## 3. Status

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Draft | Content may change. Implementation is welcome but tracking changes is at your own risk |
| Review | Open comment period; adoption is decided after discussion in Issues |
| Accepted | Adopted. No further changes that alter meaning (except trivial fixes) |
| Superseded | Replaced by a newer IHR, referenced in the header |
| Withdrawn | Withdrawn, with the reason stated in the header |

## 4. Process

1. **Proposal**: copy `docs/IHR-TEMPLATE.md`, write a draft, open a Pull Request
2. **Acceptance & numbering**: the Editors check formal requirements and merge as Draft with a number
3. **Comment period**: discussion in Issues. Feedback from implementations (e.g., the Family series and registry operations) carries special weight
4. **Adoption**: the Editors confirm consensus — not unanimity but **rough consensus** (no sustained objections)
5. **Revision**: changes to an Accepted document are made as a new IHR; the old one becomes Superseded

## 5. Document requirements

- Japanese is the primary language; an English abstract (or full translation) may be attached for international reach
- Markdown format; diagrams embedded as Mermaid code blocks
- Each IHR starts with a header table: number, title, status, date, author, license
- Normative keywords (MUST / SHOULD / MAY) follow RFC 2119 where needed

## 6. Editors

- The Editors manage the repository, assign numbers, and judge adoption. The initial Editors are the project founders
- Editors do not represent the interests of any particular organization. Transfer to a neutral governing body (e.g., a consortium) is planned as participation grows

## 7. License and defensive publication

- All IHRs are published under CC BY 4.0. Submitting a Pull Request constitutes agreement to this license
- Each release (tag) receives a DOI via Zenodo, providing third-party proof of the publication date
- No party is expected to obtain exclusive rights (e.g., patents) over the content of this series. Important designs are documented as IHRs promptly, as defensive publications

Names such as "ICF Hub" may be registered as trademarks to protect the quality and trust of the specification (open spec, protected name). Trademark status does not affect the availability of the specification under CC BY 4.0.
